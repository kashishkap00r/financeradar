About · Finance Radar

A calm way to read Indian finance

One feed from 300+ sources, ranked twice a day by two AI models, with no ads, no tracking and no engagement bait.

The idea

Finance Radar started as a personal frustration with how financial news is consumed in India. Every morning, the routine was the same — open eight browser tabs, scroll through three Telegram groups, check YouTube for new takes, skim Twitter for market chatter. By the time you'd gathered your morning brief, it was lunch.

So I built the thing I wished existed: a single, calm feed that pulls from 300+ sources across news desks, independent voices, institutional research, academic papers, Telegram channels, YouTube, and Twitter — curated not by algorithms, but by whether I'd actually trust the source with my own portfolio decisions.

There's no engagement bait. No push notifications. No "trending" section designed to spike anxiety. Just updates from sources that do real reporting, real analysis, and real thinking about Indian and global finance.

Every hour, an automated pipeline fetches RSS feeds and pages from newspapers, blogs, research houses, journals, YouTube channels, and Telegram channels. Articles are de-duplicated, grouped by similarity, and filtered through 180+ noise patterns to strip out clickbait, market-movement filler, and rewrites.

Twice a day, four AI models from different Gemini generations — 2.5 Flash, 3.5 Flash, 3.8 Flash and 3.1 Flash-Lite — rank the feed separately. Articles picked by two or more of them rise to the top: a consensus signal that something matters, not just that someone rewrote a press release.

The homepage opens with The Big Stories: the AI groups news, Telegram reports, YouTube explainers, tweets, and research that cover the same underlying event into one story, with a one-line summary and the best take first. A thin line ties each story's sources together, so you can see at a glance which coverage belongs to which story. The top three get the full treatment; the rest open in place when you tap them.

No personalisation. No tracking. The same feed for everyone, every time.

What's in the feed

The homepage blends the AI-ranked best of everything into one page; the tabs let you go deep on any single kind of source, newest first.

  • HomeThe Big Stories, then the best of the feed in between a few dedicated sections: Research Reports, Slow Reads (every long-form post this week from independent writers — The Ken, Finshots, The Core, Musings on Markets and more), Watch, From the Source (straight from RBI, SEBI, PIB and other official channels), Voices from Twitter, and Who Said What — the most telling quotes from the week's stories, with who said them.
  • NewsIndian and global desks — ET, Mint, Business Standard, BusinessLine, The Hindu, ThePrint, The Core — alongside Reuters, Bloomberg, the Financial Times, the WSJ and The Economist. Four desk filters: India Desk, World Desk, Indie Voices (Finshots, The Ken, The Morning Context, The Arc, Not-Ship, Carbon Brief, Our World in Data, Rest of World, Musings on Markets…), and Official Channels (RBI, SEBI, PIB, MoSPI, ECB, ADB, FRED) for the primary source rather than someone's take on it. In focus shows the stories several outlets are covering.
  • TelegramSeven curated finance and brokerage channels, rendered as clean, readable posts instead of an endless scroll. Filter to research reports or plain posts, or hide price-target calls.
  • ReportsInstitutional research from 20+ publishers — brokerages and banks (HDFC Securities, Axis Direct, Goldman Sachs, UBS, SBI, ING), rating agencies (CRISIL, CareEdge), policy bodies (NITI Aayog, CSEP) and energy and climate researchers (IEA, IEEFA, Ember, CEEW). Indian or international, your pick.
  • PapersAcademic and policy research from 55 sources — RBI, IMF, World Bank, NBER, EPW, ORF, ICRIER, NIPFP, Carnegie India and leading economics and finance journals — for when you want the rigorous version of a debate.
  • YouTubeFinance explainers and market commentary from 30+ channels, filterable by Traditional Media, Indie Voices, or Educational/Explainers.
  • TwitterCurated market chatter from investors, analysts and newsrooms, split into a High Signal lane that surfaces only the best takes and a Full Stream for everything else.

About the site

Finance Radar is built entirely with Python, vanilla HTML/CSS/JS, and a healthy amount of Claude. No React. No framework. No build step. The whole site is one generated page, one stylesheet and one script, served as static files via Cloudflare Pages.

The AI ranking runs twice daily using four Gemini models (2.5 Flash, 3.5 Flash, 3.8 Flash and 3.1 Flash-Lite), explicitly prompted to favour depth over speed, explanation over opinion, and data over anecdote. The ranking models see the same feed you do — they have no inside information.

A few things you might like: bookmarks on every article, report, video, tweet and quote (copy them all as a formatted list in one click) and keyboard shortcuts (J/K to move through the news, / to search, H for Home and 1–6 for the tabs).

This is a personal project by Kashish Kapoor. It's not a startup. It doesn't have funding. It doesn't need your email. It exists because I wanted a better way to read financial news, and maybe you do too.
